# M1-F.A Manufacturing and Product Feasibility Discovery Plan

Status: planning and evidence-collection preparation only. No supplier, product, manufacturing capability, production configuration, regulatory approval, or commercial readiness has been verified.

## Objective

Prepare an evidence-based path for deciding whether AROMAN can eventually offer two distinct product lines in Iran:

- **B — AROMAN Signature Collection:** AROMAN-branded finished perfumes produced by a qualified partner.
- **C — AROMAN Controlled Personalization:** customer choices limited to a future documented and manufacturer-approved physical production system.

This plan does not approve either line for sale, select a supplier, or establish that any manufacturing method is available.

## Relationship to M1-E and M2-0

M1-E approved B and C as product directions, identified Iran as the intended initial market, and kept the launch sequence unresolved. M1-E also separates a creative concept, an advisory suggestion, and an approved production configuration. Studio note selections and Smart Perfumer output remain conceptual; neither is a manufacturing specification.

M1-F.A prepares the research artifacts for **M2-0: Commercial decisions and source verification** in [the existing roadmap](M1E1_IMPLEMENTATION_ROADMAP.md). M2-0 remains the decision and evidence gate: confirm owner decisions, identify real sources, verify documents and samples, and keep commerce off until the applicable product-line evidence exists. M1-F.A itself performs no supplier research or outreach.

## Independent feasibility workstreams

### B — Signature Collection

Establish whether specific finished AROMAN-branded SKUs can be lawfully sourced or produced, consistently supplied, packaged, labeled, priced, stored, delivered, supported, and replenished. Evidence must be SKU- and market-specific. A capable manufacturer alone does not establish that a particular product, brand right, label, price, or fulfillment promise is approved.

Key unknowns include manufacturing/private-label model; product and brand ownership; actual samples and approval criteria; product identity and batch documentation; quality and traceability records; packaging sourcing; minimum order quantities; capacity and repeatability; written pricing and lead times; labeling/claims responsibilities; storage and transport; defect, return, and replenishment handling; and Iran-specific legal, regulatory, tax, and operational review.

### C — Controlled Personalization

Establish whether a qualified partner can support a constrained, reproducible physical customization system, and whether its options can be documented, versioned, reviewed, controlled, quoted, produced, and withdrawn safely and operationally. This is separate from allowing free creative exploration in Studio.

Key unknowns include whether an approved physical method or option set exists; who defines and authorizes it; which customer choices are supportable; how configuration versions map to controlled manufacturing documents; how quality/repeatability and safety/labeling reviews are performed; whether per-order production is possible; capacity, minimum batch, pricing, lead time and fulfillment; confidentiality/IP ownership; and how withdrawn options are blocked and historical orders handled.

### Why the gates are separate

B concerns identifiable finished products and their SKUs, supply, packaging, stock, and product-specific evidence. C concerns an approved, versioned production system, permitted customer options, configuration control, and capacity at the time of a quote/order. Evidence for B does not establish C readiness; evidence for C does not establish B readiness. Either line can remain unavailable while the other is evaluated. **No sequence is approved:** the product owner must decide whether B may launch before C, whether both launch together, or whether neither launches until further evidence.

## Proposed evidence sequence

This sequence is a planning recommendation. Research, contact, sampling, or approval does not begin automatically under M1-F.A.

1. **Owner scope and decision record:** confirm intended customer, geography within Iran, candidate product scope, B/C launch-sequence decision, decision owners, and what evidence would be sufficient for the next gate.
2. **Candidate identification:** only in a separately authorized research slice, identify real organizations using attributable primary sources. Record source date, exact claims, scope, and unanswered questions. Do not rank or approve candidates from marketing copy alone.
3. **Document request and authentication:** request current, product/method-specific manufacturing, quality, ownership, packaging, capacity, and operational documents. Record who supplied each item, version/date, applicability, and independent verification steps. “Received” is not “verified.”
4. **Independent checks:** have qualified reviewers authenticate relevant records and assess scope, validity, consistency, and Iran applicability. Escalate missing, contradictory, expired, or unverifiable evidence; fail closed.
5. **Samples/prototypes:** only after owner authorization and appropriate agreements, define written sample criteria, handling, reviewers, and acceptance record. A sample review does not by itself establish safety, legal approval, repeatability, or commercial readiness.
6. **Operations and commercial evidence:** establish written terms, ownership, cost and price authority, capacity, packaging, warehousing, delivery, returns/support, replenishment, traceability, and contingency/withdrawal procedures.
7. **Product-line-specific go/no-go:** update [the readiness matrix](M1F_PRODUCTION_GO_NO_GO.md) with evidence links and named approvers. Advance only the line and scope that independently satisfy its gates. Escalate unresolved owner and jurisdiction decisions instead of assuming them.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Website or verbal claims are mistaken for verified capability | Attribute every claim; request current written evidence; independently authenticate and record reviewer, date, and scope. |
| A demo Studio concept is treated as a production formula | Keep concept, advisory, and production-configuration systems separate; do not map raw note IDs to purchasable options. |
| B evidence is generalized to C or vice versa | Maintain separate registers, evidence links, approvers, and release decisions for each line. |
| Missing or contradictory records are interpreted optimistically | Use “Not verified,” fail closed, preserve gaps, and require explicit resolution before advancing. |
| Product/sample review is confused with safety or regulatory approval | Assign appropriately qualified reviewers and separate acceptance records and authorities. |
| A partner or configuration is no longer available | Require version, effective dates, capacity source, change notice, suspension/withdrawal, and customer-order handling procedures. |
| Confidential formulation or customer data is over-collected | Collect only necessary evidence under reviewed confidentiality/access controls; keep sensitive manufacturing files out of public frontend APIs. |
| Market requirements are assumed from general knowledge | Obtain current primary sources and qualified Iran-local advice; do not make unsupported compliance claims. |
| Launch sequence is assumed | Keep B-before-C unresolved until the product owner records a decision based on separate evidence. |
