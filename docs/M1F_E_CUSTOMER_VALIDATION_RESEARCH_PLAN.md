# M1-F.E — Signature Collection B Customer Validation Research Plan

- **Status:** Internal research planning baseline; execution not authorized
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Product line:** B — AROMAN Signature Collection
- **Intended market:** Iran
- **Provisional planning ceiling:** 25,000,000 Iranian toman
- **Spending authorized:** No
- **Supplier outreach authorized:** No
- **Customer recruitment authorized:** No

## 1. Purpose and boundaries

This plan proposes directional early-stage customer research to examine AROMAN's proposed premium-niche positioning, three creative territories, hypothetical price levels, and discovery preferences. It is not a statistically representative market forecast, a product-validation certificate, or a commercial-release gate.

No fragrance has been formulated, manufactured, sampled, tested, approved, or made available for purchase. Research descriptions must say clearly that concepts are descriptive only. This plan does not authorize research execution, spending, recruitment, supplier contact, product development, sampling, manufacturing, or sales.

The product direction and boundaries are recorded in the [Signature Collection product brief](M1F_SIGNATURE_COLLECTION_PRODUCT_BRIEF.md). B and C retain separate production-readiness gates in [M1-F Production Go/No-Go](M1F_PRODUCTION_GO_NO_GO.md).

## 2. Research objectives

If separately authorized, the study should:

1. Understand how fragrance enthusiasts in Iran discover unfamiliar brands and make fragrance purchase decisions.
2. Test comprehension and initial response to the proposed premium-niche positioning.
3. Evaluate clarity and appeal of the three existing internal creative territories.
4. Assess reactions to hypothetical price stimuli without treating responses as validated prices.
5. Explore preferences for discovery and sampling experiences that are not currently offered or approved.
6. Identify trust barriers, missing evidence, objections, and reasons participants might reject the proposition.

## 3. Proposed participants

**Proposed primary profile:** Adults aged 18 or older in the intended Iranian market who purchased fragrance or actively explored a fragrance purchase during the previous 12 months.

If recruitment is later approved, seek variation in fragrance experience, purchasing budgets, and preferences rather than recruiting only highly engaged niche-fragrance enthusiasts. Exclude research-team members and people whose involvement presents a conflict of interest. Participation should be voluntary and based on clear consent. Collect only data needed for the approved research purpose.

This profile and recruitment approach are proposals, not evidence that the audience is reachable, representative, or commercially attractive.

## 4. Proposed research stages

All stages below are proposals. They require the governance approvals in Section 7 before any external data collection, recruitment, communication, paid service, or incentive.

### Stage A — Desk market benchmark

Create a dated, reproducible snapshot of comparable Iranian fragrance retail listings near the time of any later fieldwork. For each observation, record:

- Brand and product name.
- Seller and public listing reference.
- Listed size/volume.
- Listed price in toman and capture date.
- Visible discount/promotion status and visible stock status, if shown.
- Domestic or imported classification, with the basis recorded.
- Reason the listing is considered comparable to the proposed positioning.

Do not invent benchmark values. Do not treat advertised prices as completed transactions, reliable stock, or evidence of willingness to pay. Preserve the observation date because listings and exchange or market conditions may change. Resolve inconsistent size, seller, or discount information before including a listing in comparisons.

### Stage B — Customer discovery interviews

**Proposed target:** 12–18 voluntary interviews with adults matching the proposed participant profile.

Explore recent fragrance purchases and decision processes, discovery channels, motivations, attitudes toward unfamiliar brands, perceived originality, trust signals, budgets and price expectations, importance of sampling, and reactions to AROMAN's proposed story. Begin with open-ended questions before showing creative concepts or price stimuli to reduce anchoring.

The proposed count is qualitative and directional. It is not a statistically representative sample and should not be used to estimate market prevalence.

### Stage C — Creative concept evaluation

Use neutral Persian descriptions of the three existing working territories:

- **B-01 — Clear Signal:** fresh, aromatic, mineral.
- **B-02 — Velvet Tension:** floral, textured and contrasting.
- **B-03 — Slow Ember:** warm, woody and atmospheric.

Randomize order where practical. Ask about clarity, appeal, remembered differences, imagined occasions, trust and reasons for interest or rejection. Tell participants the descriptions are creative concepts only; no corresponding physical fragrance exists for evaluation. Do not ask participants to rate real smell, longevity, projection, safety, formulation quality, or performance.

### Stage D — Directional pricing survey

- **Proposed sample:** 90–150 respondents.
- **Hypothetical reference product:** 50 mL finished fragrance.
- **Proposed initial price stimuli:** 4,500,000; 6,000,000; and 8,000,000 toman.
- **Core price hypothesis:** 6,000,000 toman; proposed and not validated.

If approved, randomly assign each respondent one initial price stimulus while keeping other product information consistent. Potential measures include perceived expensiveness, credibility, affordability, conditional purchase consideration, trust, and information needed before making an actual decision.

With 90–150 participants across three price arms, each arm would have roughly 30–50 respondents if allocation is balanced. This is a small directional sample: it cannot establish statistically representative demand, precise price elasticity, an optimal selling price, or actual purchase behavior. Hypothetical purchase questions are vulnerable to stated-preference bias and the absence of a real product, transaction, and trade-off. Report counts and uncertainty; do not convert interest responses into sales forecasts.

All stimuli are research hypotheses only. They are not manufacturing-based costs, validated market prices, approved retail prices, or a price commitment.

### Stage E — Discovery-experience research

Compare hypothetical preferences for a discovery set, in-person testing, smaller individual sizes, and a full-size purchase. Ask what concerns each format might address and what evidence participants would want before choosing it.

No sampling or discovery format is approved, technically or legally established, or currently offered. Concept descriptions must not imply that real samples exist.

## 6. Proposed analysis and outcomes

If research is later authorized and completed, prepare a concise internal report covering:

1. Recruitment method, participant profile at an appropriately aggregated level, and study limitations.
2. Repeated qualitative themes, including disagreement and counterevidence.
3. Positioning comprehension, perceived differentiation and trust barriers.
4. Relative clarity and appeal of the three creative territories.
5. Price-stimulus responses with counts, uncertainty, and hypothetical-bias caveats.
6. Discovery-experience preferences and objections.
7. Recommendations, unresolved questions and evidence that challenges the preferred direction.

Possible internal recommendations are to refine positioning, revise creative concepts, reconsider pricing hypotheses, or consider a separately authorized next feasibility decision. These are recommendations for owner review, not automatic approvals.

Self-reported interest is not actual demand. Concept-description feedback does not validate a physical fragrance. No study result automatically authorizes supplier contact, product development, sampling, manufacturing, a purchase offer, or launch.

## 7. Research governance approvals required before execution

The product owner must separately approve and record:

- Final research scope and instruments, including Persian-language copy.
- Recruitment method and any external participant communication.
- Participant consent, eligibility, privacy notice, and withdrawal handling.
- Data minimization, storage location, retention period, access controls, and deletion process.
- Named research owner, interviewer, analyst, and decision reviewer.
- Any incentives, paid tools, services, or other spending, with explicit limits.
- Analysis method, reporting format, handling of contradictory findings, and final decision authority.

Do not store participant names, contact details, raw identifiable responses, or restricted research materials in the public repository. Use a separately approved secure process if research is authorized. The 25,000,000-toman ceiling is not permission to incur any cost.
