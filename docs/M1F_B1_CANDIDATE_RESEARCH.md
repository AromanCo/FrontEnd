# M1-F.B.1 Iranian Manufacturing Candidate Research

**Status:** public-source desk research only. This document does not recommend, qualify, approve, or rank a supplier. No organization was contacted; no form was submitted and no email was sent.

**Access date:** 2026-10-23. Site page dates are recorded where visible; otherwise publication/effective dates were not shown. “Claim” means the organization’s own public statement, not independently established capability. Candidate-level evidence and unknown fields are tracked in the [Partner Evidence Register](M1F_PARTNER_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md). The separate B/C [production approval gates](M1F_PRODUCTION_GO_NO_GO.md) remain unchanged and **Not verified**.

## Decision context and method

M1-E approved the intended Iran market and two product directions: B, AROMAN Signature Collection finished perfumes, and C, AROMAN Controlled Personalization. The sequence of any commercial launch remains a product-owner decision. M1-F.A treats those directions as separate evidence and approval workstreams. Studio concepts and Smart Perfumer suggestions are not formulas, production instructions, or purchasable configurations.

This review examined the eight initial research leads using their public websites and, where a page was not directly accessible, the search-indexed page attributable to that domain. Public website claims were recorded as third-party claims. No registry search, facility inspection, certification authentication, document request, sample review, reference check, or local legal/regulatory review was performed. Search-index summaries were used only to locate or record public page content, not as independent verification.

## Candidate findings and traceable sources

All sources below were accessed on 2026-10-23. Where source pages contain dated posts, the observed publication date is included. A company’s own claims remain self-published regardless of how specific they are.

### 1. Pishro Tejarat Maham

The [company page](https://maham-trading.com/about-us/) describes Pishro Tejarat Maham as a Tehran-based perfume-product manufacturer and trader in associated raw materials. It says the company’s new plant in Shamsabad Industrial City began operation in 2023. The [private-label and contract page](https://maham-trading.com/private-label-contract-manufacturing/) claims private-label production of essences, perfumes, deodorants and cosmetics in Iran, and describes formulation, permits, production and packaging as services. The page also includes broad cosmetics/personal-care copy, so the actual perfume production and client scope must be isolated in later verification. The [home page](https://maham-trading.com/) lists product brands and chemical/component supply sections.

**Role assessment:** stated finished-perfume manufacturer, private-label/contract contractor, raw-material/component supplier, and consulting provider. **B follow-up:** yes, if authorized, to establish current facility, perfume-specific scope, brand rights, quality evidence, actual samples and written terms. **C follow-up:** only to ask whether any bounded, version-controlled and approved physical personalization method exists; no evidence of one was found. **Limitations:** plant, operating status, permits, quality, capacity, customer references and private-label service claims are not independently verified. MOQ, sampling terms, available capacity, lead time, labeling responsibility and IP/formula ownership are not documented in reviewed pages.

### 2. Sarv Sepid Asia / SARVCORP

The [home page](https://www.sarvcorp.com/) describes SARVCORP as supplying essential oils to health and cosmetic factories and links to production, contract manufacturing, raw-material supply, design, branding and product development. Its [contract manufacturing page](https://www.sarvcorp.com/%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%AF-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF%DB%8C/) says Sarv Sepid Asia provides contract manufacturing for perfume/fragrance brands and companies, and claims laboratory, QC, formulation staff, industrial lines, customizable volume/packaging and OEM/private label.

**Role assessment:** stated fragrance/raw-material supplier, perfume contract manufacturer and product-development/branding provider. **B follow-up:** yes, subject to legal identity and facility confirmation. **C follow-up:** inquiry only; the public materials reviewed do not specify an approved, bounded customer-choice system or its version/change controls. **Limitations:** detailed public-page text was sparse/inconsistently extractable. Precise legal entity, address, operating location, service availability, QC records, capacity, packaging and client IP arrangements remain unknown. The page’s promotional claims about high volume and quality are not evidence of AROMAN capacity or qualification.

### 3. Shams Cosmetics Laboratories

The [home page](https://shamscosmetics.com/) offers private-label/free-capacity cosmetics and personal-care products under a customer’s brand and lists perfume, cologne, body splash and related fragrance products. It also publishes claims concerning GMP, CE, ISO 22716, formula licensing and government permissions. Its [about/policy page](https://shamscosmetics.com/%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%87-%D9%85%D8%A7/) displays additional license claims that have not been authenticated. The [private-label production page](https://shamscosmetics.com/private-label-production/), dated 2023-12-25 and updated 2023-12-31 on the page, claims contract/private-label production, formulation, permissions, testing, packaging and label design. The home page includes general quality-system and permission claims; they have not been authenticated with issuers. The page identifies an Eshtehard, Alborz address.

**Role assessment:** stated cosmetics/personal-care laboratory and private-label contractor that includes perfume/cologne in its claimed scope. **B follow-up:** yes, for perfume-specific feasibility, not inferred from other cosmetics categories. **C follow-up:** ask only whether a restricted, documented, approved option system exists; private-label production alone is insufficient. **Limitations:** some service posts are 2021 and 2023. Certificate and license claims require current scope/issuer/site verification. No MOQ, fragrance-specific sample protocol, available capacity, lead time, labeling allocation, or IP terms were found.

### 4. Ashtad Mehr / Ettehad Ashtad Mehr Development and Trade Holding

The [home page](https://ashtadmehr.com/) and [services page](https://ashtadmehr.com/services/) claim custom branded perfume/cologne and fragrance essence production. The [laboratory page](https://ashtadmehr.com/laboratory/) describes production/simulation of perfume essences and related products. Its [about page](https://ashtadmehr.com/about/) claims a Tehran registration in 2010 and also describes locations in Istanbul, Dubai, London and Tehran, with the main facility in Istanbul.

**Role assessment:** stated finished fragrance and essence producer, with a multi-industry holding/group identity. **B follow-up:** yes, conditionally, after determining the contracting entity and exact production site. **C follow-up:** inquiry only; no controlled production configuration, permitted option model, or withdrawal controls were found. **Limitations:** the multi-location/plant descriptions do not establish which entity or site would manufacture an AROMAN item. Registration and facility claims have not been independently checked. Quality documentation, packaging/labeling, MOQ, samples, capacity, repeatability, lead times and IP are unknown.

### 5. Ettehad Perfume Co.

The [home page](https://ettehadperfume.com/) describes manufacturing, importing fragrance and production supplies, organizational scent design, and factory consultation. It states that products are made under client names. The [production page](https://ettehadperfume.com/production/) describes raw-material selection, formulation, production, packaging and storage; it lists a Dubai address and UAE telephone number and its footer states “This is a staging environment.” The [about page](https://ettehadperfume.com/about-us/) discusses Iranian market activity and importing Mane products in Iran.

**Role assessment:** stated finished perfume/cosmetic/hygiene product maker, client-name production provider, importer/distributor and factory consultant. **B follow-up:** conditional on resolving entity, location and the staging-site issue. **C follow-up:** exploratory only; no constrained approved option/version system evidenced. **Limitations/contradiction:** an Iran-facing narrative is paired with Dubai contact/location and an explicit staging disclaimer on a production page. Do not infer an Iranian manufacturing operation. Current corporate identity, legal entity, facilities, client production, and whether content is operational must first be independently confirmed. All commercial and quality fields are unknown.

**Role assessment:** stated consumer perfume brand/retailer and claimed own-brand/third-party custom producer. **B follow-up:** conditional; first verify whether third-party manufacturing remains active and identify factory location. **C follow-up:** not established; the described custom work may be individually bespoke and does not evidence Controlled Personalization. **Limitations:** About copy is old and the Tehran address is a contact/retail address, not proof of a factory. No public, current manufacturing documentation or commercial terms were found. Other registry/directory claims were not treated as independent evidence in this pass.

## Evidence quality, recency and limitations

- The strongest evidence available in this pass is attributable first-party web copy describing the organization’s own claimed role. It is not independent manufacturing evidence.
- Some sites have undated copy, old posts, or contradictory/unclear experience statements. Mersana’s About page contains placeholder text; its home page contains conflicting “4 years” and “10 years R&D” statements. Jove’s production claims appear in content indexed as approximately five years old. Shams has relevant posts dating from 2021/2022 and 2023. No archived history or live facility state was established.
- Sarv pages were accessible but some exposed little text in browser rendering. The contract-manufacturing page’s indexed content was available and is linked; legal identity/location should be confirmed before relying on it.
- Ettehad Perfume’s page explicitly labels itself a staging environment and gives Dubai contact information, while other copy refers to Iran. This is an unresolved identity/geography issue, not a conclusion that the claimed production is false.
- Jove’s homepage timed out on one direct open, although indexed page content and About/contact pages were accessible. This is a source-access limitation, not proof of site inactivity.
- No evidence was collected from authorities, certificate issuers, customers, independent auditors, site inspections, samples, or signed commercial documents. No third-party directory claim was used to qualify any supplier.
- For every candidate, MOQ, sampling/prototype process, available capacity, repeatability evidence, quality documentation, packaging/labeling responsibilities, formulation ownership/IP, and lead times are unknown unless stated only as a company claim above. Prices and displayed product availability were not used to infer AROMAN supply.

The following groupings only describe which questions the public pages make reasonable for a later, authorized verification phase. They are not ordered by preference.

### B — Signature Collection document-verification questions

### C — Controlled Personalization discovery questions

No candidate’s reviewed public material establishes the required approved bases/accords/options, permitted-combination controls, configuration versioning, production repeatability, withdrawal controls, or product-line-specific safety/label/operational approvals.

- **Potential discussion only if authorized:** Pishro Tejarat Maham, Sarv Sepid Asia, Shams Cosmetics Laboratories, Ashtad Mehr, Ettehad Perfume Co., and Mersana Tejarat Isatis, because their claims touch perfume production, formulation, development, or custom work. For each, ask whether any constrained system actually exists; do not treat private label, bespoke creation, or a laboratory as evidence of C readiness.
- **Bespoke distinction to resolve:** Jove Perfume and Mersana describe individualized/custom perfume activity in public copy. Such activity may be fully bespoke and remains outside C unless a manufacturer-approved constrained option model can be evidenced.
- **Input-supply-only question:** Arsa Kimya could be considered only as a potential ingredient supplier if Iran import/supply and relevant product scope are first established. It is not evidence of a C production configuration.

## Proposed M1-F.B.2 verification sequence

M1-F.B.2 should be separately authorized before any outreach. A prudent sequence is:

1. **Owner scope gate:** confirm whether verification may contact candidates, which candidate records to pursue, intended B/C scope, and who may review confidential documents. Do not assume B-before-C.
2. **Identity and geography:** independently establish legal entity, ownership/relationship to web brands, current operating facility, and contracting/manufacturing jurisdictions. Resolve Ettehad Perfume’s staging/Dubai/Iran ambiguity; Ashtad Mehr’s multiple sites; and any other group/brand distinction.
3. **Current service and product-specific scope:** request written confirmation whether perfume production for third-party brands is current; distinguish finished-perfume, component/raw-material, consulting, retail, OEM/private-label, and bespoke roles.
4. **B document set:** for comparable B candidates, request current facility/site evidence, product-specific manufacturing and quality documentation, sample and approval workflow, batch traceability/repeatability, packaging/label responsibility, rights/ownership terms, written MOQ/capacity/lead time and quote inputs. Verify claims with issuing bodies and qualified reviewers.
5. **C capability screen:** ask for documented method, controlled option boundaries, named configuration authority, version/change/withdrawal controls, representative samples and repeatability evidence. If no such system exists, record C as not demonstrated; do not retrofit a bespoke or Studio process into C.
6. **Cross-border/input branch:** only if owner-approved, assess Arsa Kimya’s ingredient-supply relevance, documentation, export-to-Iran feasibility and qualified local review. Do not treat a 45-country export claim as Iran availability.
7. **Independent review and gate update:** qualified manufacturing/quality, Iran-local legal/regulatory, safety/labeling, IP and operations reviewers assess evidence. Update the evidence register with the source, issuer, date, scope, verification method and result. Keep all M1-F production gates Not verified until their separate evidence and approval criteria are actually met.
